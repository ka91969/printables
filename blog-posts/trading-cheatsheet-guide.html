Day Trading and Crypto Cheatsheet Guide: Four Pages Explained

« Back to all articles

Trading markets is one of the riskiest activities many people try, and a reference sheet cannot remove that risk. What a good cheatsheet can do is keep the vocabulary, formulas and risk rules in front of you so that decisions are made from a written plan rather than from memory or emotion. The Day Trading and Crypto Cheatsheet covers price action, technical indicators, derivatives and risk management in four printable pages. It is designed for day traders, crypto scalpers, swing traders, options strategists and technical analysts. It is a single digital PDF sized for US Letter, and it is listed at €5.00 on its product page. This guide explains each page and sets out the cautions that matter most.

Trading involves substantial risk, including the possible loss of all capital, and leverage increases that risk. This guide is educational and is not financial advice. Test any strategy on historical data and in a demonstration account before risking real money.

Page one: candlestick patterns and price action

The first page covers reversal and continuation candles, including hammers, shooting stars, bullish and bearish engulfing bars, morning stars and dojis. It also covers market structure, with break of structure, change of character, liquidity sweeps and fair value gaps. Volume and order flow concepts such as climax volume, volume delta, point of control and the value area high and low appear here too. The page closes with strategy maps for two trader personas: crypto scalpers working on one to five minute charts, and swing traders working on four-hour to daily charts. Patterns describe what happened in a price bar. They do not predict what happens next, so always read them in context.

Page two: technical indicators and Pine Script

The second page covers indicators with their formulas and common levels, including RSI with a 14-period setting, MACD, Bollinger Bands, VWAP and ATR with a 14-period setting. It includes copy-ready Pine Script version 5 code for a custom RSI divergence detector and an intraday VWAP band envelope, along with a Fibonacci retracement grid that marks the 0.618 to 0.65 golden pocket and the 1.272 and 1.618 extensions. Any script should be read, understood and tested before it is used. Code that appears correct on a chart can still behave unexpectedly, so check its output against your own observations.

Page three: options Greeks and derivatives

The third page covers the five core Greeks, delta, gamma, theta, vega and rho, with their trader impacts. It includes derivatives formulas such as the Black-Scholes approximation, a crypto delta-neutral hedge and put-call parity, along with covered call, iron condor and long straddle setups and the Greeks bias of each. Options can expire worthless, and selling options carries obligations as well as rights. Understand assignment and exercise rules before trading any structure, and read the Greeks together rather than in isolation.

Page four: risk management and execution math

The fourth page is the most important for protecting capital. It covers fixed-fractional position sizing, the Kelly criterion, the 1 percent maximum risk rule, daily drawdown limits, buffers against revenge trading and journaling routines. It also covers crypto derivatives mechanics, including funding rates, liquidation prices and the difference between cross and isolated margin. The page also addresses common behavioural pitfalls such as moving stop-losses, chasing moves out of fear of missing out and over-leveraging. Many traders lose money through these habits rather than through poor analysis, so treat this page as the foundation of your plan.

Building a written trading plan from the sheet

Use the sheet to write a plan before you trade. State the markets and timeframes you will use, the setups you accept and the conditions required before entry. Set the size of each position using the risk formula on page four, along with the maximum loss you will accept per trade and per day. Define when you will stop trading for the day. Keep the plan beside your screen, and review it each session. A plan that is read before each trade is more likely to be followed than one kept only in memory.

Keeping a journal and testing honestly

Record each trade with its setup, entry, stop, target, outcome and a note on how you felt. Review the journal each week, looking for patterns in the setups that work and the times that cause losses. Test any pattern or indicator on historical data first, then on a demonstration account. Many approaches look convincing in hindsight and disappoint in real time, so honest testing is the only reliable guide.

Printing and using the sheet

Print on standard US Letter paper. The layout is ink-friendly, so black and white printing is usually enough, though colour can help with the chart examples. Keep the sheet next to the trading terminal or on a second display, where the risk rules are visible at the moment of decision. If you prefer a tablet, the PDF opens in GoodNotes, Notability and Xodo, where you can annotate the setups you test.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is risking too much on one trade. The one percent rule exists to prevent a losing streak from ending a trading career, so apply it strictly. A second mistake is trusting a pattern name without context or confirmation. A third is using leverage without calculating the liquidation price first. A fourth is ignoring the behavioural rules on page four, because emotion often breaks the plan before the analysis does.

Is the Day Trading and Crypto cheatsheet right for you?

The sheet suits traders who already understand the basics and want a compact reference for patterns, indicators, derivatives and risk rules. It is not suitable as a first introduction to markets, and it does not provide trading signals or guaranteed approaches. For a beginner, learning about risk and diversification from a qualified source comes first. At the time of writing the sheet is listed at €5.00.
